## Travel

Travel is one of the main ways I reset perspective. Conferences, trains, museums, mountains, different cuisines, and unfamiliar routines all seem to force a more attentive mode of living than ordinary weeks do. I especially like travel that combines a strong sense of place and culture with room to walk, think, and observe.

The 'nature' versus 'nurture' dichotomy also rears its head into travel, with nurture playing the role of human cultural experiences or urban travelling. I think the world is a beautiful place with a lot to offer - I have been both awed by breathtaking mountain views, and deeply immersed in the intricacies of local cultures - so I refuse to be categorized in this manner.

## Books

I like books that reward rereading: philosophy, literary fiction, history of ideas, and science books that are actually written well. I am less interested in consuming a high volume of books than in finding the ones that permanently sharpen how I think, although one usually goes through many books to find such gems. Reading also teaches you patience and the ability to carefully analyze ideas through rereading, skills that I think are waning these days and which everyone should develop. 

I would also characterize myself as a slow but deep thinker, meaning that I really need to take my time absorbing and critically appraising ideas before they materialize in my mind. Only after I know the idea inside and out can I begin to understand its implications. Reading lets me do this, and that is why I love reading so much.

## Movies

Film scratches a different itch than books. I tend to enjoy movies with a strong atmosphere, visual patience, clear stylistic point of view, or strong character development rather than a purely plot-driven spectacle. That is not to say that a good plot wont rope me in though. Let it be known that I am also a sucker for science fiction, even if it normally would not be the best of movies.

With the rise of streaming services, less people are attending screenings in cinemas worldwide, which is a real shame because the cinema is the optimal way to  experience any movie. Deluxe surround sound systems, large high-resolution screens, and perfect geometry await those who venture to the cinema; films shot for IMAX screens are particularly recommended. Beyond these (obvious) benefits, there is just something so magical to me about heading to the cinema, grabbing an oversized bag of popcorn and soda, and finding a cozy place to view the spectacle.

## Opera

Opera is one of those art forms that feels slightly absurd until it suddenly feels indispensable. The plots may not be overly complex but the emotional force delivered by the actors through their singing and the beautiful classical music that accompanies it is second to none. 

It is really a shame that opera audiences have been declining over the years, but many opera companies have been trying their best to attract new, young faces to their productions. This comes in the form of (typically) heavy discounts for under 35's, so I encourage all people - but in particular young people - to experience the beauty of opera!

Some of my favourite operas:
- Carmen, Bizet
- Die Zauberflöte, Mozart
- Don Giovanni, Mozart
- La Traviata, Verdi
- Le Nozze di Figaro, Mozart
- Madama Butterfly, Puccini
- Tosca, Puccini

## Art

Museums and galleries are among my favourite places to wander. Whether it be contemporary art or medieval art, everything has something unique to say which will captivate you if you let it. I love all art, but I find myself enjoying modern impressionist and expressionist paintings along with renaissance masters such as Caravaggio and Michelangelo. I also love the absurdity and lack of spatial perspective of medieval art. Contemporary art can be hit or miss for me and part of its beauty is my lack of expectation for what I will see, but when I see a piece I really like, it will hold my attention for longer than the visit.

## Painting & Sketching

I absolutely love artistic expression, especially in the form of painting, so one day I thought: "let's see what I can create." That amounted to mostly abstract painting since mastering painting requires immense dedication and a specific mode of expression that I simply cannot produce at this moment in time. However, I find bringing abstract figures to life very amusing and an excellent way to unwind. I have so far produced two works:

![](/assets/images/Paintings&Sketches/IMG_3899.jpeg)
![](/assets/images/Paintings&Sketches/IMG_3950.jpeg)

with many more on the way. One aspect of abstract painting that I find fascinating is how boldly it reflects the progress of human evolution. Modern humans started painting images of their direct perception without much regard for realism, then during the renaissance they refined their techniques to accurately portray the natural world. Then came new forms of expression which depicted nature and the human condition in a way that only vaguely resembles our perceptions, ultimately leading to the current state of painting: images almost complete divorced from reality. It is still an open issue when (if such a question actually makes sense to ask) exactly human consciousness and higher level reasoning came into the fore, but this transition is surely captured in our ability to imagine forms which could only have come from minds that have transcended natural evolution.
