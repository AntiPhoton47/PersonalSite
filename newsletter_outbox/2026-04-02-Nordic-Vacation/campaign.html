My Nordic Adventure

A research visit to NORDITA mixed with travel through Sweden and Norway

Read the full post

I just got back from an extended nordic vacation where I visited Stockholm, Gothenburg, and Oslo. This was all on the back of an invitation I received from a collaborator to visit the Nordic Institute of Theoretical Physics (NORDITA) so we could push a couple of the projects we have going on currently.

It was my first time visiting Scandinavia, which has been on my bucket list for a long time, and I was particularly impressed by the laid-back atmosphere of Sweden and Norway. The people I encountered were all very friendly and there was a real sense of life enjoyement among them (possibly due to the saunas littered everywhere). All three of the cities I visited had ample green space and major waterfronts, which was reminiscent of my hometown Toronto that resides on one of the great lakes: lake Ontario.

Stockholm in particular is like this lake archipelago sandwiched between two large land masses, with each area having its own charming personality. Upon my initial arrival in Stockholm, I explored the Norrmalm district, which is where the central train station T-centralen is located.

NORDITA sits in the heart of the University of Stockholm campus, with a bridge linking it to the rest of the physics department. During my stay there, I was introduced to some interesting researchers working on topics ranging from quantum gravity to sphere packing. In fact, one of the researchers working on sphere packing is the grandson of one of my grandmother's closest friends; what a small world!

The main project that my collaborator and I worked on was this idea of getting a reinforcement learning agent to perform quantum information tasks that were implicitly embedded in spacetime, and have the structure of spacetime appear in the agent's memory after training. I wont go into the complexity associated with the term "agent," but you can roughly think of it as something embedded in a world that can interact and influence this world, and which has the capability to make choices concerning its actions. This agent we envision has no prior understanding of what spacetime is nor do they see the connection to the abstract quantum information task: they start out blindly exploring, eventually mastering the task by building a representation of the spacetime structure that the environment is embedded in.

Outside of work I spent most of my time hanging out with some of the master's and PhD students from NORDITA. We spent many hours discussing and debating in pubs, the complexities of the world, and shared many laughs together too.

I also managed to visit the Stockholm opera house one evening to catch a performance of Madama Butterfly by Puccini.

After my research visit in Stockholm I took the train to Gothenburg and stayed there for about two days. Although not as vibrant as Stockholm, Gothenburg still has some interesting history and spots.

I then travelled to my final destination - Oslo - after my short sojourn in Gothenburg. Of all the places I visited during this trip, Oslo was my absolute favourite!
