My love of opera is a somewhat recent development that began shortly into my master's degree at Waterloo. It evolved principally from my admiration of classical music, which I was getting more into as a means for concentrating background music. Ironically, a lot of opera melodies and arias are quite striking and not the greatest background companions but I grew to really appreciate the technical aspects of the craft; not to mention that some of the most iconic pieces ever written were created for operas. Pieces such as Nessun Dorma by Puccini, Largo al factotum della citta by Rossini, or L'amour est un oiseau rebelle by Bizet are firmly embedded in the public conciousness. 

The first opera I went to see was Carmen by Bizet, which was at the Four Seasons Theatre in downtown Toronto and was performed by the Canadian Opera Company. Carmen was (and is still) a magnificent opera with many famous pieces such as L'amour est un oiseau rebelle and Toreador's Song. It is one of the best operas you can see as an introduction to opera due to its sweeping sets and grand soundtrack, and from that moment on I was hooked. I immediately drafted up a list (as I usually do) of operas and opera houses I wanted to see, and started to regularly attend operas wherever I could. 

Moving to Innsbruck to pursue my PhD in physics was essentially a gift from the opera gods. Opera traces its history back to Italy, becoming extremely popular and spreading to all corners of Europe during and after the renaissance. As you can imagine then, opera is endemic in Europe with virtually every city possessing an opera house and some of the most famous theatres being present there. Probably the most prestigious of those theatres is Teatro alla Scala in Milano, which brings me to the story I wanted to tell.

I had been dying to visit Teatro alla Scala ever since moving to Europe but I either already had plans or could not find any tickets. Due to its status, tickets sell out very fast - often in just a couple days after going on sale - so last November I chose an Italian opera and marked down the date when the tickets first went on sale; I was determined to get there. I almost didn't get any tickets but I managed to snag some cheaper seats in a booth next to the stage. Outside of the opera I made my way to many different art galleries in Milano, seeing more experimental art at Pirelli Hangar Bicocca (a former factory) and medieval/renaissance art at Poldi Pezzoli museum. I was particularly impressed with the collection at Museo del Novecento, which had some great futurist paintings by Boccioni. I of course also made the somewhat expensive pilgrimage to see the Last Supper by da Vinci, which was made even more sublime thanks to the careful explanation of my tour guide.

I also had a cup of coffee at the Wes Anderson designed retro diner in the Fondazione Prada and visited the Santuario di San Bernardino alle Ossa, which is a famous ossuary containing walls filled with human skulls.
